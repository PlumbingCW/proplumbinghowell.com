About Ardena

Ardena is an unincorporated community on County Route 524 near Vanderveer Road. Reference sources describe the area as roughly half farmland and half residential development. Howell Township also has an Ardena Elementary School.

We could not find a map of exact boundaries or a published record of building years, so we do not claim either. If you are not sure whether your address counts as Ardena, call and say where you live.

Know your water and wastewater

Public water and sewer do not reach every property in a township the size of Howell. Some homes use private wells, and some use septic tanks. We have no verified source that says which Ardena streets use which, so check your own bills and ask the township or utility.

If your house is on a private well

  • Test the water on the schedule your health department recommends.
  • Know where the pump, pressure tank and shutoff are.
  • Call if pressure drops or the pump cycles constantly.
  • Tell your plumber the system type at the start.

If your house is on public water

  • Know where the meter and main shutoff are.
  • Watch the meter for movement when everything is off.
  • Ask the utility about its service line records.
  • Call about sudden pressure or flow changes.

New Jersey's Private Well Testing Act requires a property with a private well to be tested when it is sold, and landlords must test every five years. Required checks include nitrates, pH, iron, manganese, lead, arsenic and others. A treatment system can be considered if the results warrant it.

Septic systems and what a plumber can and cannot do

If you suspect a septic problem, such as slow drains in every fixture, odors in the yard or a soggy area over the tank, a licensed septic professional should inspect the tank and field. We help with the plumbing side, such as clearing drains inside the house, and our drain cleaning page explains how. New Jersey regulates septic systems under N.J.A.C. 7:9A, so permits and design go through the proper agencies.

Outbuildings, hose bibs and freeze protection

Properties with barns, detached garages or workshops often run a water line out to the building. A line like that needs its own shutoff, and an outdoor spigot should be drained and closed before the first hard freeze. A frozen line can split without any sign until it thaws, so check exposed pipes if you have been away during a cold snap.

Before a hard freeze

  • Disconnect hoses and close the shutoff to each outdoor spigot.
  • Drain exposed lines in unheated buildings.
  • Know where the main shutoff is and keep the path to it clear.
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls during extreme cold.
  • If a pipe has frozen, do not use an open flame to thaw it.
