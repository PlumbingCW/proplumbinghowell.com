DIY Leak Detection for Howell, NJ Homeowners

You can narrow down many leaks yourself with a water meter test, a toilet dye test and a short walk around the house. These checks cost almost nothing and take about an hour.

This guide walks you through each test in order, explains what the results mean and tells you when it is time to call a plumber with acoustic and thermal tools. It is written for Howell, NJ homes on a public water meter or on a private well.

Step 1: the meter test

The water meter is the best leak detector you have. Turn off every tap and appliance in the house, including the ice maker and the irrigation system. Find the meter, usually in the basement, a utility room or a ground box near the street, and read the dial or look at the small leak indicator.

Wait thirty minutes without using water, then read it again. If the numbers moved or the indicator spins, water is leaving the pipes somewhere. If the meter did not move, you may still have a small leak that shows only when water is under pressure for longer.

Shut off all water use

No taps, no washer, no dishwasher, no irrigation.

Read the meter

Note every digit or the dial position, or take a photo.

Wait

Leave the water off for at least thirty minutes, or longer if you can.

Read again

Any change means a leak exists on the house side of the meter.

Step 2: is it inside or outside the house

Close the main shutoff valve inside the house and look at the meter again. If the meter keeps moving with the main closed, the leak is between the meter and the valve, which usually means the underground service line or the piping at the meter. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house or in a branch line beyond the valve.

Other signs to write down

  • Water stains or bubbling paint on walls or ceilings.
  • A musty smell in a closet or cabinet.
  • Warped floors or baseboards.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure.
  • A wet spot or soft ground in the yard.
  • A bill that rises with no change in use.

Step 4: the ice and refrigerator line

A refrigerator water line can leak behind the unit for weeks. Pull the refrigerator out carefully, look at the connection at the wall and the line to the back of the unit. A slow drip at a compression fitting is a common source. Replace worn lines and shut off the valve if you find a leak.

When to stop and call a plumber

If the meter moves and the quick checks do not explain it, a plumber should take over. A hidden leak behind a wall, under a floor or in the ground needs acoustic listening, thermal imaging or a pressure test to pinpoint it. Our leak detection page explains how those tools work. If a pipe has already split, read about burst pipe repair.

Do not cut into drywall to look for a leak without a plan. Opening the wrong spot adds repair work and may not find the source. Also avoid chemical leak sealers poured into the system, since they can clog valves and fixtures.

Keep a simple leak log

If you suspect a slow leak, write down the meter reading at the same time every evening and every morning for three days, with a note of any water used. A pattern of readings that climb overnight when no water was used is strong evidence of a leak, and the log gives a plumber a head start on where to look. Photographs of stains with the date make the story clearer still.
