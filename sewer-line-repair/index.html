Signs of a damaged sewer line

A single clog is one thing. A line that clogs again and again, drains slowly in every fixture or smells in the yard is another. The pipe itself may be failing.

Common warning signs

  • Several drains slow or gurgling at once.
  • Sewage or damp odors in the basement or yard.
  • A soggy, unusually green patch of lawn along the pipe path.
  • Foundation cracks or a sinking walkway near the line.
  • Mice or insects entering through a broken pipe.

Repair options, explained honestly

There is no single best repair. The right choice depends on what the camera shows, how deep the pipe runs and what sits on top of it.

We also read the camera for belly sections where the pipe sags and holds water. A belly does not always need a repair, but it explains slow drains and may justify jetting on a schedule.

Cast iron, clay and plastic: what we find in older lines

Older house lines may be cast iron, clay or Orangeburg fiber pipe, which flakes and collapses. Cast iron scales and thins from the inside. Clay cracks and admits roots at every joint. We identify the material on camera and plan the repair around it. If cast iron drain pipes inside the house are failing, the work may extend into the house, which our repiping team can discuss.

Howell note: who is responsible and what to ask

Responsibility for the sewer lateral varies. In general the homeowner owns the pipe from the house to the point where it joins the main, but the exact boundary depends on the system. Howell Township, the largest municipality in Monmouth County at about 61 square miles, is served by different sewer arrangements in different areas, and some neighborhoods rely on septic systems.

A 2015 news report on Freewood Acres, off Route 9 near I-195, described a long-running septic problem and a sewer connection that reached only some of its 620 lots.

Before any dig, ask the Howell Construction Office what permits apply. Its plumbing subcode official enforces the state code at the municipal building on Preventorium Road. If your property is on septic, tell us, since a failed septic line is a different repair from a failed sewer lateral.

Standards and permits for sewer work

Sewer repairs are plumbing work under the state subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code. Camera findings in the industry are commonly coded under the NASSCO PACP system, and we give you the footage so a second opinion can use it.
