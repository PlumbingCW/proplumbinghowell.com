Restaurants and food service

A restaurant kitchen puts grease, food solids and hot water into the drains all day. A grease trap or interceptor catches part of it, and the trap needs regular service. When the kitchen line backs up in the middle of dinner service, the cost is lost sales. We clear the line, look at the cause with a camera, and suggest jetting on a schedule if grease keeps returning. Our hydro jetting page explains how that works.

Offices, shops and rental buildings

Offices and shops see steady fixture use but few emergencies. The usual calls are a running toilet, a slow restroom drain, a water heater that no longer keeps up, and a leak above a ceiling tile. Landlords and property managers also call for repeated clogs in a shared stack, where one tenant's problem becomes another tenant's flooded floor.

Howell note: permits and who to ask

Commercial plumbing work in Howell can require a permit, depending on scope. The township's Construction Office, and the plumbing subcode official who works there, enforce the state code from the municipal building on Preventorium Road. Replacing a fixture in kind usually differs from moving a drain line or adding a fixture, so ask before the work starts.

New Jersey adopted the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code as its plumbing standard, and a licensed plumbing contractor should be doing any permitted work. If your lease says the landlord handles a given system, confirm who authorizes the repair before you call us.

Code that applies to business buildings

Commercial work follows the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code, and the township Construction Office decides what needs a permit. We tell you in advance, and we do not start permitted work without one.
