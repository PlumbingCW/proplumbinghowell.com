Sewer Camera Inspection in Howell, NJ

A sewer camera inspection in Howell, NJ sends a waterproof camera through your drain line and shows what is inside the pipe on a monitor. You see the roots, cracks, bellies and breaks, and we record the footage for you.

Homebuyers order inspections before closing. Homeowners order them after repeat clogs, and landlords order them before they buy or lease a property. In each case the goal is the same, which is to replace guessing with a picture.

What the camera can show you

Root intrusion

Fine roots at joints or thick masses that block the pipe, and where they enter.

Cracks and offsets

Hairline cracks, separated joints and sections that no longer line up.

Bellies

Low spots where water and waste sit, which explain slow drains and repeat clogs.

Scale and grease

Buildup that narrows the pipe even when it is not fully blocked.

Pipe material

Cast iron, clay, PVC or fiber pipe, which tells you what to expect.

Collapses

A pipe that has caved in and needs replacement.

Inspection before you buy a Howell home

A home inspector looks at the visible plumbing. The sewer line runs underground and is rarely covered. A camera inspection fits between the inspection contingency and the closing date, and it can reveal a repair the seller never mentioned. If the camera shows damage, you can negotiate the price or ask for a credit.

Camera work is also useful if the house is on septic. We check the line to the tank and tell you if the line is intact, while a septic professional should inspect the tank and field.

When a camera is not enough

If the line is blocked solid, we clear it first with a cable or a jetter so the camera can pass. Our sewer line cleaning page explains that step, and hydro jetting covers heavier buildup. If the footage shows damage, we move to the repair options on our sewer line repair page.

We also keep the video if you want a second opinion. A buyer's attorney, a seller or an insurer can look at the same footage and agree on what it shows.

Howell note: ask about the connection

Howell is the largest municipality in Monmouth County at about 61 square miles, and not every street has public sewer. If you buy in a neighborhood that has long used septic systems, ask the township and the seller whether the house is on sewer or septic and whether any sewer project is planned. A 2015 report on the Freewood Acres section described a gradual shift from septic to a regional sewer line.

For any repair that follows, the township's Construction Office and its plumbing subcode official, at the municipal building on Preventorium Road, can tell you which permits apply.

How to read a sewer inspection

Municipal and contractor reports often use the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program coding standard for cracks, roots, offsets and breaks. We explain each finding in plain words and hand you the video, so you can share it with a buyer, a seller or an insurer.
