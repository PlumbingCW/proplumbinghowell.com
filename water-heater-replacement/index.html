When a failed tank is an emergency

A slow weep at a fitting can wait a day. A tank that leaks from its seam, floods a basement or finished room, or sits in a closet above drywall is an emergency, because every hour adds water damage. Turn off the cold inlet first, then the gas control or breaker, and open a hot tap to relieve pressure.

Signs the heater needs replacing, not repair

  • Rust colored hot water or flakes at the tap.
  • Water seeping from the bottom of the tank or the seam.
  • A tank older than about 10 to 12 years with repeated problems.
  • Loud rumbling that returns right after a flush.

Choosing the replacement that fits your house

The cheapest unit is not always the least expensive one to own. A tank replaced like for like is the fastest swap and often the lowest upfront cost. A tankless heater saves floor space and can supply continuous hot water, but it needs the right gas line, venting and a scale plan. A heat pump hybrid uses far less electricity and needs room and airflow.

Howell note: permits and code

Water heater replacement in New Jersey falls under the Uniform Construction Code, and the plumbing subcode adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code, according to N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. In Howell, the township Construction Office, which includes a plumbing subcode official, enforces it from the municipal building on Preventorium Road.

Permit rules differ for a like for like swap and for a change in fuel or venting, so ask the Howell Construction Office before work starts. New Jersey law also says plumbing must be done by a licensed master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their W-2 employees, and licensees must show their license number on invoices and vehicles. Ask for it.

Replace now or keep repairing?

If the tank is under eight years old and the problem is a part, a repair usually makes sense, and our water heater repair page explains how we decide. Past about 12 years, a repair buys little time. A new unit also resets the warranty and can lower the monthly gas or electric bill.

Code, permits and carbon monoxide

New Jersey adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and a replacement may need a permit that the township Construction Office decides. The CDC advises battery operated or battery backup carbon monoxide detectors near every sleeping area, and annual service of heating systems and water heaters that burn gas or oil.
