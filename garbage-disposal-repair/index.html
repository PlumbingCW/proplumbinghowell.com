Why a disposal jams or hums

A hum with no spin usually means the flywheel is stuck. A fibrous item, a bone, a fruit pit or a piece of metal can lock it. Most disposals have a hex socket in the bottom for a wrench to turn the flywheel back and forth, and a red reset button on the underside after the motor overheats. If those steps do not free it, the motor or the wiring may be at fault.

Safe steps before you call

  • Turn the power off at the wall switch and the breaker.
  • Look into the sink with a flashlight and remove anything you can see with tongs, never with your hand.
  • Use the hex socket on the underside to turn the flywheel.
  • Press the red reset button after the motor cools.
  • Run cold water and test once the jam is clear.

Leaks: where the water is coming from

A leak from the bottom of a disposal means the housing has cracked or the seals have failed, and replacement is the usual answer. A leak at the top is often the sink flange or its putty. A leak at the drain pipe connection is often a loose slip nut or a worn washer. We trace the water to its source before we say what needs replacing.

Repair or replace

A disposal that jams once from a dropped utensil is fine to reset. A unit that is rusting, leaking or years past its useful life is not. A new unit costs more than a repair, but it spares you the next failure. We help you pick a size and a feed type that suits how you cook, and we discuss whether you actually need one. What not to put in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, pasta, rice, potato peels, celery, bones and fruit pits cause clogs and jams. Cold water during use and for a short time after helps flush the line.

Howell note: older kitchens and septic homes

Many Howell homes were built in different decades and kitchen drain lines vary. A disposal sends more solids into the drain, which can bother older lines and, on a septic property, the tank. Howell Township covers about 61 square miles, and some neighborhoods rely on septic systems, as a 2015 report on the Freewood Acres area near Route 9 and I-195 described.

If your house is on septic, ask your septic professional whether a disposal suits your system before you install or keep one. If the sink drain backs up past the disposal, our drain cleaning team can clear the line.

Wiring, permits and septic rules

Disposal wiring falls under the National Electrical Code, NEC, and drain work under the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Septic systems are covered by the state's separate standards for individual subsurface sewage disposal systems, N.J.A.C. 7:9A, so ask your septic professional before you add a disposal.
