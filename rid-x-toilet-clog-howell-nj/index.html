Rid X and Toilet Clogs: A Howell, NJ Homeowner Guide

Rid X is sold as a septic tank treatment that adds bacteria and enzymes to a tank. It is not marketed as a drain opener, and it will not break up a solid clog sitting in a toilet trap. If your toilet is clogged, a plunger or a closet auger is the right tool.

This guide explains what Rid X does and does not do, how to clear a toilet clog safely and what a repeat clog can mean for your septic system in Howell, NJ.

What a septic treatment is meant to do

A septic tank holds household waste while solids settle and bacteria break some of it down. Products like Rid X add enzymes and bacteria to the tank. They are intended as a regular maintenance product for the tank and not as a way to open a blocked pipe. Septic professionals disagree on how much benefit these products offer, and you should read the label and ask your septic professional.

A toilet clog is a different problem. A mass of paper, a toy or a wipe lodged in the trap has to be moved or broken apart physically. Pouring a bacteria treatment into the bowl will not do that, and it takes time to work even in a tank.

How to clear a toilet clog safely

Stop the flushing

Do not flush again. Close the supply valve behind the toilet if the water is rising.

Plunge properly

Use a flange plunger with a good seal and push and pull with steady strokes.

Use a closet auger

If plunging fails, feed the auger into the trap and crank until the clog breaks or comes free.

Test

Flush once with the supply on and watch the bowl drain.

Skip the chemical cleaner

Chemical drain openers can burn skin, damage the toilet and make a later plumber visit unsafe. They also harm the bacteria in a septic tank that you are trying to protect. If the clog will not clear, call a plumber.

When a repeat clog points past the toilet

One clog is bad luck. Frequent clogs, gurgling and slow drains in several fixtures point to a problem in the line or the tank. A full septic tank, a blocked outlet baffle, a saturated drain field and roots in the line can all push waste back toward the house. The toilet shows it first because it is often the lowest or the largest fixture.

Signs the problem is the system, not the toilet

  • More than one fixture is slow or gurgles.
  • Water rises in a tub or shower when you flush.
  • A sewage smell in the yard or near the tank.
  • Wet or unusually green ground over the drain field.
  • The tank has not been pumped for years.
  • The clog returns within days.

For a line problem, our sewer line cleaning page explains how we clear and camera a house line. For the fixture itself, see toilet repair. We handle the house line, and a septic professional handles the tank and the field.

Habits that prevent toilet clogs

Simple rules for any house

  • Use a moderate amount of toilet paper and flush twice if needed.
  • Keep wipes, cotton products and paper towels out of the toilet.
  • Install a toilet with a good flush rating if yours clogs often.
  • Teach children what can and cannot go in the bowl.
  • Keep a flange plunger within reach in every bathroom.
  • Fix a weak flush before it becomes a clog.

Toilet paper is built to fall apart in water. Most other products are not. The common cause of a stubborn clog is a wad of material that never breaks down, and once it settles in the trap, the next flush packs it tighter. Treat any product that is not paper or human waste as trash.

Septic care that works better than additives

Pumping the tank on a schedule, spreading out laundry loads, fixing leaking fixtures and keeping grease and chemicals out of the drain protect a septic system more than most additives do. Keep a record of each pumping and any repairs, and ask your septic professional how often to inspect the tank and the outlet baffle.

Howell note: why septic matters here

Howell Township covers about 61 square miles, and sewer service is not the same across it. New Jersey American Water says it operates the Adelphia Wastewater Collection System in Howell, which serves about 4,100 accounts. Many other properties use private septic systems, and a 2015 report on the Freewood Acres section, off Route 9 near I-195, described septic systems alongside a sewer connection that reached only part of its 620 lots.

Ask your septic professional how often to pump your tank. If you do not know whether you are on sewer or septic, ask the township before you buy a treatment product. Plumbers in Lakewood and across the area ask the same question when a toilet will not stay clear.
