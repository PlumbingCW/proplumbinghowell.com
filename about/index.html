How a call to us works

When you call (732) 466-1098, a person answers and asks what is happening, where you are and whether water is running right now. If water is flowing, we tell you how to close the main valve while we arrange a plumber. Then we give you an arrival window for the next available technician. We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic, weather and earlier calls change it.

Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes. We take your name, address and a short description of the problem so the plumber arrives knowing what to bring.

What we promise and what we do not

What you can expect from us

  • A clear price before work starts.
  • A plain explanation of what failed and why.
  • Honest advice on repair versus replacement.
  • Same day appointments when a plumber is available.
  • Cleanup of the work area when we finish.

What we will not do

  • Start work before you agree to the price.
  • Push a replacement when a repair will do.
  • Invent an emergency to raise the bill.
  • Promise a result before we have seen the problem.
  • Claim reviews, awards or credentials we cannot show.

The work we do

Our core work is urgent and routine plumbing for houses and small businesses. That means drain cleaning, water heater replacement, water heater repair, leak detection, sewer line work and burst pipe repair. We also install fixtures, replace old pipe and service sump pumps and boilers. The full list is on our plumbing services page.

Where we work

We are based on Route 9 in Howell Township, the largest municipality in Monmouth County by area at about 61 square miles. Its neighborhoods, such as Ramtown, Oak Glen and the Route 9 corridor around Howell Commons, spread across a large area. That is why a dispatch line that sends the next plumber in range works better than a fixed schedule. We also cover Lakewood and the towns on our service area page.

Howell note: why local knowledge changes the advice

Howell does not have one water or sewer system. Parts of the township are served by New Jersey American Water, which also operates the Adelphia Wastewater Collection System in Howell, and other properties rely on wells and septic systems. The right advice on a disposal, a backup or a water heater depends on which one serves your house.

We ask which system you use at the start of the call. It takes ten seconds and prevents advice that fits a different house. If you are not sure, the township or your utility can tell you, and we can help you find the cleanout or the tank.

What to expect on the day

The plumber calls or texts when they are close, finds the problem and explains it before touching anything. You see the failed part or the camera footage when there is one. You get a clear price for the repair and, when it applies, a second price for replacement, so you can choose. Work starts when you say so. When it ends, the plumber tests the repair, cleans up and tells you what to watch for in the next few days.

If the fix is bigger than a single visit, we explain the plan in steps and tell you what you can safely use in the meantime. If you want a second opinion, we understand and will give you the findings in writing.

Licensing and insurance

Plumbing work in New Jersey must be done by a licensed plumbing contractor, and permitted work is inspected by the township. We work through licensed plumbers and we carry insurance.

According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs, plumbing contractors in New Jersey are licensed through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. If you need proof of licensing or insurance for a landlord, a lender or an insurer, ask when you call and we will tell you what we can provide.
