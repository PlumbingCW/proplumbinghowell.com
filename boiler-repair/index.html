Why a boiler stops heating

Boiler symptoms and likely causes

Safe checks before you call

  • Make sure the thermostat is set to heat and has fresh batteries.
  • Check that the boiler has power and the switch is on.
  • Look at the pressure gauge, which usually sits between 12 and 15 psi when cold, but follow your manual.
  • Check that the fuel supply is on, and that oil tanks are not empty.
  • Do not open the boiler cabinet or bleed gas lines yourself.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility.

Gas and oil boilers

Gas boilers use burners, ignition parts and a vent. Oil boilers use a burner, a nozzle and a fuel pump, and they need regular cleaning. Both have safety controls that shut the boiler down when something is wrong. A shutdown is the boiler protecting the house, and we find what triggered it before we restart it. If it needs an oil burner service or fuel work, we tell you early if another trade should handle that part.

Repair or replace

An older boiler with a cracked heat exchanger, repeated leaks or parts that are no longer made is not worth repairing again and again. We explain the trade offs in plain terms, including the cost of a repair against the age of the unit. If replacement is the answer, we describe the options and a clear price, and we cover the work that falls under plumbing installation such as new valves and piping.

Howell note: fuel and seasonal timing

Howell homes heat with a mix of fuels and systems depending on age and street, so tell us whether your boiler runs on natural gas, oil or propane. A gas boiler depends on a working gas supply, and an oil boiler depends on a filled tank and a clean burner.

Cold snaps bring a rush of calls, and parts get hard to find. If your boiler is old, have it checked in early fall, before the first cold week. Carbon monoxide alarms should be present near sleeping areas, and replace the batteries each year. Permits may apply to a boiler replacement, and the township Construction Office, at the municipal building on Preventorium Road, can tell you what applies.

Carbon monoxide and annual service

According to the CDC, you should install battery operated or battery backup carbon monoxide detectors near every sleeping area and have heating systems, water heaters and other gas or oil burning appliances serviced by a qualified technician every year. The CDC also suggests replacing detectors every five years or per the maker.
