Freewood Acres sits on both sides of Route 9, just north of the Interstate 195 interchange in western Howell Township. It is best known for small residential lots and a long, well documented change in how its wastewater is handled.

Where Freewood Acres is

Freewood Acres is an unincorporated community inside Howell Township. U.S. Route 9 runs through its center, and Georgia Tavern Road also crosses the area. Land O'Pines Elementary School and Manasquan Reservoir County Park lie to the east, and the township runs its own Freewood Acres Park with a basketball court, playground and open space.

The homes are mostly single story houses laid out on a street grid. Boundaries are not formally mapped in the sources we found, so we describe the area by its anchor roads, Route 9, Interstate 195 and Sunnyside Road, instead of drawing lines.

From septic tanks to public sewer

For decades these small lots relied on septic systems. Local news reporting says odor complaints reached the township by 2013 and that some systems were failing. Howell designed a sewer plan for the neighborhood back in 1988, work picked up again in 2015, and construction began in October 2018.

Township ordinances set aside $9.26 million for sewer mains on both sides of Route 9 between Interstate 195 and Sunnyside Road. A further $3.45 million covered mains and connections for northbound Route 9 between West Farms Road and Casino Drive. The main construction contract went to Lucas Brothers, and later changes raised its cost to about $11.9 million.

Local note: what connecting to sewer involves

Reporting on the project said residents would receive a notice from the township giving them one year to connect to the new system. It also said some residents were using affordable housing funds to help with costs.

We could not confirm from public sources which lots are connected today. Ask the township whether your address has been notified, and get your own deadline in writing. If you are unsure which system your house uses, tell us when you call, because it changes how we clear a drain.

What this means for your plumbing

Switching from a septic tank to public sewer is a plumbing job as well as a utility project. The house drain has to be routed to the new connection, and the old tank may need to be closed out under local rules. A permit may apply, and the township construction office is the place to confirm that before work starts. New Jersey enforces the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code under N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.

General guidance for older small lots

We are not claiming that every house here has a particular pipe or drain problem, because we have no records that say so. What we can say is that any drain that backs up more than once deserves a camera look, and that roots, belly sections and cracked pipe are the usual causes of repeat backups in any age of house. Our drain cleaning page explains how that works.

If your house is near the end of a line that was recently tied in, ask us to note where your cleanout is. A known cleanout location makes the next service call faster. Another part of Howell, Adelphia, has a documented sewer system, and our Adelphia page explains how its operator describes it.
