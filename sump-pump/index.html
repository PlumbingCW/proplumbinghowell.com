Why a sump pump fails

Most failures come from a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a failed motor or a lost power supply. A pump that runs nonstop can mean a float jam, a failed check valve or water coming in faster than the pump can move it. One that never runs may be unplugged, tripped or dead.

Backup power and backup pumps

Storms that bring heavy rain often bring power loss. A battery backup or a water powered backup keeps the pit emptying when the main pump cannot run. A second pump placed higher in the pit gives more capacity. We explain the choices and what each needs, such as a dedicated outlet or a plumbing connection.

Test your sump pump before storm season

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump start.
  • Listen for odd noises, grinding or rattling.
  • Check that the discharge pipe is clear and drains away from the house.
  • Make sure the pump is on its own outlet, ideally a GFCI one.
  • Test the battery backup if you have one.
  • Clear debris from the pit.

Where the discharge goes

A sump pump moves water outside, away from the foundation. If the discharge pipe is blocked, frozen or aimed at the foundation, the water returns and the pump runs forever. In winter, an exposed pipe can freeze and cause a backup. We check the route and fix what we can.

Howell note: ask before you discharge

Howell Township covers about 61 square miles, and groundwater and drainage conditions vary from street to street. A basement that stays dry in one house can flood in the next, so a pump that suits your neighbor may not suit you.

Where a sump pump discharges is a local question. Some areas restrict connections to sanitary sewer or to a neighbor's property. The township Construction Office, at the municipal building on Preventorium Road, can tell you the rules for your street. Never connect a sump pump to a sewer cleanout, and tell us if your home is on septic, since extra water can overload the system.

Drying out afterward

The EPA's guidance on mold is to fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible and dry all items completely. After a pump failure, that means pulling wet items out of the basement, running fans and a dehumidifier and watching for damp odors.
