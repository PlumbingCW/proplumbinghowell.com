About Ramtown

Ramtown is an unincorporated community in the southeast corner of Howell. According to Census figures, it covers about 2.2 square miles and held 2,130 housing units in 2020, with only 1.2 percent vacant. Wall Township lies to the east and Brick Township to the southeast, and the North Branch of the Metedeconk River forms part of its southwest border.

The area includes Ramtown Elementary, Greenville Elementary and Howell Middle School South. Ramtown-Greenville Road is one of its named roads, and the Ramtown Fire Company, District 4, serves the surrounding part of the township.

What the sources do not say

The public figures we found do not give the year homes were built, and they do not say which streets use public sewer, a septic system or a private well. We do not guess. If you tell us what you have, we can plan around it, and the township or the water and sewer operator can confirm your service.

Questions to settle before a plumbing visit

  • Is your water from a public system or a private well.
  • Does the house drain to a public sewer or a septic tank.
  • Where is the main water shutoff and is it easy to turn.
  • How old is the water heater and what fuel does it use.
  • Where is the sewer cleanout, if the house has one.

Basement water near a river

Because the Metedeconk runs along the edge of Ramtown, some readers will wonder about wet basements. We have no flood records for specific streets and make no claim about them. If your basement takes on water after storms, a working sump pump and a clear discharge line help, and our sump pump page explains testing and replacement.

If a pipe is leaking right now, close the main water shutoff, open a low faucet to drain the line and call. Photographs help your insurance agent later.

Getting ready for a water heater visit

A visit goes faster when the area around the heater is clear. Move boxes and stored items back a few feet, and note where the shutoff valve and the electrical disconnect or gas valve are. Take a photo of the data label on the tank, because it shows the size, model and age.

Tell us whether the house has a power vent, a flue or a drain pan, and whether the heater sits in a basement, garage or closet. Those details shape the price and the time needed, and we explain both before work starts.
