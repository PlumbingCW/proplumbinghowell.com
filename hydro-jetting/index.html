Why a snake sometimes fails and a jetter works

A cable cuts a hole the width of the blade. The grease, scale or roots along the wall stay put, and the opening slowly closes again. We often see a line that was snaked and clogged within weeks. A jetter sprays water forward and backward through the pipe, scraping the wall and flushing the debris out the cleanout.

Roots, grease and scale: what jetting clears

Roots enter through tiny joint gaps, then thicken inside the pipe. Grease coats the wall and catches debris. Scale hardens in iron and old steel lines. A jetter handles all three when the pipe is sound. Where the pipe is cracked or collapsed, jetting can make a weak line worse, which is why we look first.

We camera the line before we jet

A quick inspection shows if the pipe can take pressure. If it is cracked, offset or has a belly, we show you the footage and talk through a repair instead of forcing water through a damaged pipe.

That inspection ties into our sewer camera inspection service, and repair choices are explained on the sewer line repair page.

Sewer backup: what to do first

While you wait for the plumber

  • Stop running water, including the washing machine and dishwasher.
  • Do not flush toilets, since the line may be full.
  • Move rugs and stored items away from the lowest drain.
  • Keep children and pets out of any wet area, because sewage water can carry bacteria.
  • Do not mix cleaning chemicals trying to clear it.

How a jetting visit runs

Locate access

Howell note: sewer or septic changes the plan

Jetting a sewer lateral only makes sense when your house connects to a public or regional sewer. In Howell, some neighborhoods have long relied on private septic systems. A 2015 report on the Freewood Acres section, off Route 9 near Interstate 195, described wells and septic systems and a sewer connection that reached only part of the 620 lots at the time.

On a septic property we look at the tank, the distribution box and the drain field before we jet anything. If the tank is full or the field is saturated, jetting the house line will not fix repeated backups, and we say so. If you are unsure which system serves you, check your records or ask the township.

How inspections describe pipe damage

Sewer camera work in the industry commonly codes defects using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program system.
