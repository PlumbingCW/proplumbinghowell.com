How to tell it is the main line and not one drain

One slow sink points to that sink. A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains, or a floor drain that fills when someone flushes, points to the main line. The lowest fixture in the house shows the problem first, because water runs downhill and backs up through the lowest opening.

Signs of a main line blockage

  • Two or more fixtures slow or backing up at the same time.
  • Gurgling sounds from a toilet or tub when another fixture drains.
  • Water rising in a basement floor drain or laundry standing pipe.
  • A sewage smell near the cleanout or in the lowest room.
  • Clogs that return within days of a cable visit.

Cable, jetter or both

A cable machine drives a spinning cutting head through the pipe. It opens a path through soft blockages and thin roots. A jetter sends high pressure water through a hose and scrubs the wall. Many jobs start with a cable to reopen flow, then finish with a camera pass to see what caused it.

Why we put a camera on the line afterward

Clearing a blockage is the symptom fix. The camera tells you the cause. If the footage shows a clean pipe with a single wipe lodged in it, you may never need us again. If it shows roots at every joint, we explain a maintenance plan or the repair choices covered on our sewer line repair page.

What not to pour down a blocked line

Do not pour drain chemicals into a backed up main line. They sit in standing water, can harm the pipe and are a hazard to the technician who has to work in the line afterward. Tell us if you already used one.

Keeping the line clear afterward

Habits that protect a sewer line

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste, even if a wipe says flushable.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds and food scraps out of kitchen drains.
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep it accessible.
  • Watch for trees planted over the line path.
  • Schedule a camera look after the second blockage in a year.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • Stop running water in the house, including the washer and dishwasher.
  • Do not flush toilets, since the line may already be full.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaners into the drain.
  • Move rugs and stored items away from the lowest drain.
  • Keep children and pets out of any wet area because sewage water can carry bacteria.
  • Take photos of any water damage for your insurance agent.

Howell note: sewer or septic, and where the cleanout sits

Howell Township covers about 61 square miles, and sewer service is not uniform across it. Some areas connect to public sewer and others, historically, have used septic systems. In the Freewood Acres area off Route 9 near I-195, a 2015 report described septic systems and a sewer connection that reached only part of 620 lots at the time.

On a septic property, a backup may start in the tank or the distribution box and not in the line to the street. Tell us which system serves your house. We clear the house line first, and if the tank is full or the field is saturated we say so rather than selling you a cleaning that will not hold.

Code and who owns what

Cleanouts exist so a line can be cleared without removing fixtures, and the state plumbing subcode governs where they go. New Jersey adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. If your house has none, we can recommend adding one while the line is open.
