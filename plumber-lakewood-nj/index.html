Emergency plumbing in Lakewood, ZIP 08701

Lakewood Township covers about 25 square miles and counted 135,158 residents in the 2020 census, which makes it one of the larger townships in Ocean County. It shares a border with Howell Township to the north, along with Brick, Jackson and Toms River. Our Howell address is on Route 9, and Route 9 also runs through Lakewood, so crews follow one main road when a call comes in from the township.

Most Lakewood calls we take are urgent ones. A pipe splits behind a wall, a main line backs up into a basement or a water heater fails on a cold morning. The sections below match each of those to what we do, and they also explain what you can do in the first ten minutes to limit damage.

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house, then open a low faucet to drain the pipes.

Protect the electric

If water is near outlets or the panel, shut off power only if you can reach the breaker while standing dry.

Stop using drains

In a sewer backup, do not flush or run the washer or dishwasher. More water makes the backup worse.

Document it

Take photos and video before moving anything. Your insurance agent will ask for them.

Old sewer mains and what they mean for your line

New Jersey American Water runs the sewer collection system in the western part of Lakewood Township. It reports about 11,000 accounts, roughly 95 miles of gravity sewer main and 14 lift stations, and it says the oldest parts of the system date back to 1890. The utility owns the main. The pipe from your house to the main is generally yours, and it can be just as old as the street it connects to.

That matters when a line keeps backing up. A cable clears the blockage, but a camera shows whether roots, a belly or a cracked section is causing it. We run the camera on repeat backups and show you the footage, and our sewer camera inspection page explains what it can find.

Lakewood note: the lead and galvanized line program

In May 2026, New Jersey American Water announced door to door lead service line assessments in Lakewood Township, carried out by its contractor CDM Smith. A 2021 state law requires water providers to identify and replace lead and galvanized steel service lines by 2031. The utility says inspections are free, staff carry photo ID badges and wear logoed vests, and no one will ask for payment.

If an inspector knocks, you can allow the visual check or schedule it online. If your plumber finds old steel pipe during a repair, tell the utility. Our water service line repair page explains how buried lines are located and replaced.

Hot water, heat and sudden failures

Water heaters fail with little warning. A tank that is rusting at the base or weeping at a fitting is near the end, and a unit that leaks should be shut off at the supply valve while you wait. We decide with you whether repair makes sense or replacement is the better call. Our water heater replacement page explains how we handle a failed tank.

The same goes for the boiler. If the house has no heat in winter and you smell gas, leave first and call the gas utility or 911. If there is no gas smell, call us and tell us your fuel type so we send the right plumber.

Lakes and creeks: why sump pumps matter more

Three lakes, Carasaljo, Manetta and Shenandoah, sit on the South Branch of the Metedeconk River inside the township, and Lake Waddill lies on Kettle Creek. Streets near lakes and creeks can see wet basements during a heavy storm, so a sump pump that fails at the wrong time matters more there.

If you live near water and your pump is more than a few years old, a test and a battery backup are worth considering. Our sump pump service page covers repair and replacement.
