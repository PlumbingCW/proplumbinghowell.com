When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow bathroom sink can wait until morning. Several fixtures gurgling at once cannot, because that pattern points to the main drain, and the next flush may come back up through the lowest opening in the house, usually a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub.

Call right away if you see any of these

  • Water or sewage rising in a tub, shower or floor drain after you flush or run the washer.
  • Gurgling from a toilet when a sink or tub drains.
  • A strong sewer smell in the basement that was not there yesterday.
  • No drain in the house will empty, even after a plunger.

If only one fixture is blocked, a plunger and a few minutes are worth trying. Skip the liquid chemical openers. They sit in the pipe above a hard clog, they can burn skin when a technician later opens the trap, and they do nothing for roots or grease further down the line.

Clogged drain, drain snaking and cabling: which one you need

People search for all three terms and usually need the same thing. A hand or electric cable breaks through a blockage in a sink, tub or branch line. A longer machine cable reaches the main line. Both are drain cleaning, and we match the tool to what the pipe is telling us.

Kitchen sink clogs get their own approach

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and builds a waxy layer that narrows the opening until a noodle or coffee ground closes it. We clear the line with a cable that scrapes the walls, then flush it with hot water so the grease does not simply move downstream.

If the clog is right below a garbage disposal, we check the disposal first. A jammed unit and a blocked drain arm look alike from above, and the fix for each is different. When the disposal itself is failing, our garbage disposal repair page covers that work.

Howell note: sewer or septic changes the diagnosis

Howell is the largest municipality in Monmouth County by area, and not every street has public sewer. A 2015 news report on the Freewood Acres section, 620 residential lots on numbered streets off Route 9 near Interstate 195, described long reliance on wells and septic systems, with only some lots tied into the Manasquan River Regional Sewerage Authority line.

That matters for drain work. On a sewered street, a main line clog is a pipe problem between your house and the connection. On a septic property, repeated backups can also come from a full tank or a saturated drain field, and cabling the line only helps if the tank is not the cause.

Ask your township or check your records to learn which system serves your home. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how we handle the lines that run beyond the foundation.

Storm drains and yard drains

Yard catch basins and downspout drains clog with leaves, silt and pine needles, and a full basin can push water toward a foundation during a heavy rain. We clear these lines with a cable or a jetter and check that the outlet runs clear. If a drain line leaks underground, you will see a soggy patch that never dries, and we trace it before anyone digs.

When a drain keeps coming back

A line that clears and clogs again within weeks is telling you something. Roots, a sagging section that holds water, or a cracked joint are the usual reasons. A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of the pipe so you can decide between jetting and a repair, instead of paying to clear the same clog again.

Where cabling cannot scrape off heavy buildup, a jetter washes the pipe wall clean with water pressure. We explain that option on the hydro jetting page, and we tell you if the pipe is too damaged for it.

The code behind drain and vent work

New Jersey adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. That code is why each fixture needs a trap and a vent. We never remove a trap or cap a vent to make a drain run, because that trades a clog for sewer gas in the house.
